# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small-business owners, plumbers, electricians, contractors, salons, landscapers, restaurants, who received a cold-email demo of *their own* business and came here to vet Frontdoor before paying. They are practical and time-poor, and many have been burned by overpriced agencies or scammy "we'll fix your website" cold calls, so they arrive skeptical. They are not designers. They skew 35 to 60+, so legibility, contrast, and obvious trust signals matter more than trend-chasing. Their unspoken questions on arrival: *Is this real? Can I trust them with my money and my online presence? Is this actually better than what I have or could afford?*

## Product Purpose

Convert skepticism into confidence and move the visitor toward checkout. Frontdoor finds businesses with outdated or absent websites, generates a beautiful custom demo of their site, and uses the demo itself as the pitch. This marketing site is the credibility layer behind that pitch.

The site itself is the single most important proof point: it is the live argument that Frontdoor builds genuinely beautiful, custom websites. If any section could ship unchanged on a competitor's template, it has failed its job. Success looks like the visitor thinking *"these people are obviously the real deal; this is better than what an agency would charge me thousands for,"* and clicking through to activate their demo.

## Brand Personality

**Audacious, plainspoken, crafted.**

- **Audacious** in design: maximal motion, ambitious typography, a "how did they make that?" reaction. The site is a flex, deliberately. It should err on the side of *too* artsy and get tuned down, never the reverse.
- **Plainspoken** in voice: no jargon, no hype, no overselling. A wary tradesperson trusts plain language and concrete claims. The visual spectacle is loud; the words are honest and direct.
- **Crafted** throughout: the taste is self-evident in the details, not announced. Warmth comes from the front-door idea, a great first impression *welcomes* the right customers in.

The defining tension, held on purpose: visually audacious, verbally plainspoken.

## Anti-references

What this must explicitly NOT look like:

- **Generic AI/SaaS landing page.** Gradient blobs, Inter everywhere, three identical feature cards, the big-number hero-metric template, purple gradients, glassmorphism by default. This is the exact "AI made that" failure the site exists to disprove.
- **Website-builder aesthetic.** Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy template galleries. This is the category Frontdoor positions directly against; looking like it would be self-defeating.
- **Corporate agency with stock photography.** Smiling-people-at-laptops, handshake imagery, anonymous enterprise polish, soulless "digital solutions" copy.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** Every section is live evidence that Frontdoor builds beautiful sites. Practice what you preach, visibly. The craft of this page is the argument.
2. **Show, don't tell.** Lead with real generated demos and before/after transformations. Never *claim* "beautiful, custom websites"; demonstrate it. The work is the headline.
3. **Make it a flex; err audacious.** Maximal motion and ambitious design are the strategy, not decoration. Start over-the-top and tune down. The wow reaction *is* the sales pitch for a design company.
4. **Spectacle serves the sale.** The artistry never obscures the path to yes. A time-poor owner must always understand the offer, the proof, and the price within seconds. Clarity and plainspoken honesty gate the spectacle, motion and ambition wrap a message that stays dead simple.
5. **Your website is your front door.** The threshold idea is the narrative spine: a website is the first thing a customer sees, and a bad one turns people away before they ever walk in. Make the visitor feel what their current site costs them and what a great first impression pays back. Conceptual, not literal door clip-art.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**: contrast ratios hold even with a committed, saturated palette; all interactive elements keyboard-reachable with visible focus.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` is load-bearing**, not an afterthought. Because heavy motion is core to the brand, the reduced-motion experience must be deliberately designed (instant or cross-fade states), never broken or empty.
- **Legible and generous for a non-designer, older-skewing audience.** No hairline-thin type at tiny sizes, no low-contrast gray-on-gray. Bold can still be readable.
- Respect color-vision differences: never encode meaning in color alone.
