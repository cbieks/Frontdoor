# Product

> Scope: this file governs the **demo landing pages** Frontdoor generates for
> prospects (Phase 0.5 / Phase 4), not the operator dashboard. Strategy lives in
> the repo-root `frontdoor.md`; this is the design-context layer impeccable loads.

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences, in priority order:

1. **The business owner (primary).** A non-technical local SMB operator (plumber,
   salon owner, contractor, dentist, mechanic) who receives a cold outreach link
   and, for the first time, sees their own business reflected back beautifully.
   They are busy, skeptical, and have likely been burned by web vendors before.
   The target emotion is **pride plus attainability**: "that is *my* business,
   better than I could have made it, and this is how it should look." That feeling
   is what converts them.
2. **Their customers (secondary).** Local consumers who would actually use the
   live site to evaluate the business and make contact. The demo must also be
   genuinely good for them: clear, trustworthy, fast to the point of contact.

## Product Purpose

Generate per-business demo landing pages so impressive, and so specifically
*theirs*, that the owner converts to a paid production site. The demo is the
sales pitch ("you, but beautiful"), built from the business's real brand, photos,
reviews, and voice. Frontdoor's moat is taste plus per-business intelligence, not
template volume: every demo must feel custom-built for that one business and never
templated. Success is a skeptical owner seeing their demo and wanting it live.

## Brand Personality

The expressed personality is **adaptive**: it is the prospect's own brand voice
(extracted from their existing site and reviews), amplified rather than replaced.
A spa demo is calm and refined; a plumber demo is bold and reassuring; a law firm
is restrained and credible. There is no single Frontdoor house voice imposed on
top.

The **constant** beneath that variance is the meta-personality: tailored,
confident, premium. Three words: **tailored, confident, premium.** Never generic,
never "AI-made," never kit-templated. Concretely, every demo holds the same craft
baseline regardless of category: generous whitespace, a confident type hierarchy,
real photography over stock, and restraint over decoration.

## Anti-references

- **The Wix / Squarespace template look.** Generic, obviously-from-a-kit layouts.
  The whole pitch dies if a demo reads as templated.
- **"AI-made" tells.** Inter or Geist used by reflex, purple-to-blue gradients,
  gray text on colored backgrounds, cards nested in cards, the rounded-icon-tile
  above every heading, gradient text, colored side-stripe borders, identical card
  grids, the hero-metric template, bounce or elastic easing.
- **SaaS landing-page cliches.** The training-data reflex for "landing page" is a
  software marketing site: three-tier pricing cards, a "Trusted by" logo wall,
  product-screenshot mockups, icon-tile feature grids, stats bands, and "start your
  free trial" or "boost your productivity" copy. None of it fits a local service
  business. A plumber needs services, real photos, named reviews, service area,
  hours, and a call-or-book CTA, not a software pitch.
- **Sameness across demos.** The cardinal sin given "adaptive per category." If a
  spa demo and a plumber demo feel like one site reskinned, we have failed.
- **The category reflex.** A demo whose palette or theme you could guess from the
  category alone (plumber to blue, spa to sage, law to navy and gold). Push past
  the first training-data reflex, and the second.

## Design Principles

1. **You, but beautiful.** Build from the business's real brand, photos, reviews,
   and voice. The wow comes from recognition, not invention.
2. **Custom by category, consistent in craft.** No house aesthetic. A spa, a
   plumber, and a law firm must feel like different sites. What is shared is the
   quality bar and the anti-slop discipline, not the look.
3. **Never "AI-made."** Every demo must pass the AI-slop test at both altitudes.
   If someone could say "AI made that," or guess the design from the category, it
   has failed.
4. **The owner is the first conversion.** The demo's primary job is to make a
   skeptical, non-technical owner want it live, by showing their own business done
   right.
5. **Real, but gated.** Functional elements are visibly "active on launch." The
   demo proves quality without handing over a usable site.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA on every demo. Real local customers include older and disabled users,
and accessibility is itself a quality signal that separates us from cheap
templates.

- Color contrast verified with actual checks, not eyeballed.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for every animation.
- Fully keyboard-navigable with visible focus states.
- Semantic HTML first; ARIA as supplement, never as a workaround.
